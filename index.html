The forms

Get it accepted first time

Every message is read by a person at GRIT. When something cannot be used, the writer has to fill in that story again. These are the reasons that happen most, and how to avoid each one.

1. English meaning missing

Why it happens: the brackets were forgotten, or the English was written somewhere else.

How to avoid it: after every message, type an opening bracket, the meaning in English, and a closing bracket. Look at each line before pressing send.

2. English meaning unclear or wrong

Why it happens: the meaning is one word, such as (help), or it does not match what was written.

How to avoid it: write a full, simple sentence in English, as if you were explaining it to a friend who does not speak your language.

3. Looks machine translated or copied

Why it happens: a translation tool or AI was used, or the text was copied from a friend or from an earlier answer.

How to avoid it: type it yourself, in your own words. Informal and short is fine. Neat and perfect is what looks suspicious.

4. Not written in the language you chose

Why it happens: the language chosen at the top of the form is not the language of the messages.

How to avoid it: check the language question before the writer starts. If they mix languages when they type, that is fine, and the form has a question for it. The main language should still be the one chosen.

5. Contains personal details

Why it happens: a real name, phone number or address was written in a message.

How to avoid it: leave them out. Write “my friend”, “my partner” or “my teacher” instead of a name, and never write a real phone number or address.

6. Same as another answer

Why it happens: the same message was used for two stories, or writers copied each other, or a form was sent twice.

How to avoid it: each writer writes their own answers on their own. Send the form once. If we ask for a story again, redo only the stories we list.

7. Too short to be useful

Why it happens: the message is something like “hi” or “ok”, or only an emoji, so there is nothing to learn from.

How to avoid it: short is fine, a few words can be a real message. Ask the writer: what would you really send to Zuzi?

8. Other

We add a short note to the email. Read it, and reply to the email if it is not clear.

Examples

These use English words so everyone can follow them. In your own language the format is the same: the message first, then the English meaning in round brackets.

Accepted hey wat time does the clinic open (What time does the clinic open?)

The message is typed the way a person really types it, and the meaning is a clear sentence.

Comes back: English meaning missing hey wat time does the clinic open

There are no brackets, so we cannot tell what it means in English.

Comes back: meaning unclear hey wat time does the clinic open (question)

One word does not say what the message means.

Comes back: personal details my friend Sipho said call him on 012 345 6789 (my friend Sipho said call him)

Real names and numbers must stay out. Write “my friend said call him”.

Check a line before it is sent

    This check runs on your phone and nothing is sent or saved anywhere. It only looks at the layout, not the language. Please practise with made-up examples, not a real person’s private words. For an English-language form, no brackets are needed.

    Before the writer presses send

    Four habits that help

    1. Check the first two answers together. When a writer has finished their first story, read their first two messages with them before they carry on. Mistakes are much easier to fix early.
    2. Tell writers that messy is fine. Short, slangy, mixed-up messages are what we need. Please do not ask them to tidy up what they wrote.
    3. Keep a private list of who has which writer code. If a code is lost or typed wrongly, we cannot match the answers to the writer.
    4. If we email you, redo only the stories we list. The writer uses the same writer code and does not need to start the whole form again.
    Care. Writers can skip any story and stop at any time. If someone tells you they are in danger or have been hurt, stop the form and speak to your GRIT contact straight away.
